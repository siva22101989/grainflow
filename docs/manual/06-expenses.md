# Chapter 6: Expenses

This chapter covers how to track your warehouse's operational expenses, organize them by category, and review your financial position.

---

## 6.1 Understanding the Expenses Page

The Expenses page helps you track money going out of your warehouse business -- salaries, fuel, repairs, and other running costs. It is separate from customer-related payments (rent and hamali), which are tracked in the Payments section.

When you open the Expenses page (**Dashboard > Expenses**), you see three summary cards at the top:

| Card            | What It Shows                                          |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Total Income    | Total amount received from all customer payments       |
| Total Expenses  | Sum of all recorded operational expenses               |
| Total Balance   | Net balance (Income minus Expenses)                    |

Below the summary cards is the expense history list.

## 6.2 Adding a New Expense

1. Go to **Dashboard > Expenses**.
2. Click the **Add Expense** button in the top right.
3. Fill in the expense details:
   - **Date** -- the date the expense occurred. Defaults to today.
   - **Category** -- select one of the predefined categories:
     - **Worker Salary** -- wages paid to warehouse workers.
     - **Petrol** -- fuel costs for generators, vehicles, etc.
     - **Maintenance** -- repairs, painting, equipment servicing.
     - **Utilities** -- electricity, water, internet bills.
     - **Hamali** -- labor charges paid to outside workers (not linked to a specific customer record).
     - **Other** -- any expense that does not fit the above categories.
   - **Description** -- a short note about the expense (e.g., "Generator diesel for March" or "Roof leak repair").
   - **Amount** -- the amount in Rs.
4. Click **Save Expense**.

[Screenshot: Add Expense dialog with all fields]

> **Tip:** Be specific in the description field. Writing "Petrol - 20 litres for generator" is more useful than just "Petrol" when you review expenses later.

---

## 6.3 Viewing Expense History

The expense list below the summary cards shows all recorded expenses. For each expense, you can see:

- Date.
- Category.
- Description.
- Amount.

### Filtering and Searching

Use the search and filter controls to narrow down expenses:

- **Search** -- type a keyword to find expenses by description.
- **Filter by Category** -- select a category to see only those expenses.
- **Filter by Date** -- set a date range to view expenses for a specific period.

> **Tip:** At the end of each month, filter expenses by the current month to get a quick summary of your operating costs.

---

## 6.4 Editing an Expense

If you entered the wrong amount, category, or description:

1. Find the expense in the list.
2. Click the **Edit** option from the actions menu (three dots icon).
3. Update the fields as needed.
4. Click **Save** to apply changes.

---

## 6.5 Deleting an Expense

To remove an expense that was entered by mistake:

1. Find the expense in the list.
2. Click the **Delete** option from the actions menu.
3. Confirm the deletion.

> **Important:** Deleting an expense changes your Total Expenses and Total Balance figures. Only delete if the entry was genuinely a mistake.

---

## 6.6 Understanding the Financial Summary

The three cards at the top give you a real-time picture of your warehouse finances:

- **Total Income** (green) -- all money received from customers (rent payments, hamali payments, outflow collections).
- **Total Expenses** (red) -- all operational costs you have recorded.
- **Total Balance** -- Income minus Expenses. If this number is positive, your warehouse is profitable. If negative, expenses have exceeded income.

Example:
- Total Income: Rs. 2,50,000
- Total Expenses: Rs. 1,80,000
- Total Balance: Rs. 70,000 (profit)

> **Tip:** Review your financial summary regularly. If the balance is shrinking month over month, look at the expense categories to see where costs are increasing.

---

## 6.7 Best Practices for Expense Tracking

1. **Record expenses daily.** Do not wait until the end of the month. Daily recording is more accurate and prevents forgotten entries.

2. **Use the correct category.** This makes filtering and reporting meaningful. If a category does not fit, use "Other" and write a clear description.

3. **Include all costs.** Even small expenses (e.g., Rs. 50 for tea for laborers) add up over time. Tracking them gives you a true picture of your costs.

4. **Review monthly.** At the end of each month, filter to that month and review total expenses by category. Look for unusual spikes.
